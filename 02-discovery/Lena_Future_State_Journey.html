Future state: keep it first, pay later, anywhere

How Lena moves from defaulting to whichever provider the checkout offers to choosing a Riverty card she can trust online and at the till.

Persona: Lena, 32, Cologne. Salaried, mobile-first, uses BNPL on most non-trivial purchases. Goal: keep money in her account until she has decided to keep the item, without choosing a provider each time.

1

Activate in minutes

User action

Verifies identity once in app → card live within minutes.

Internal state

Monitors activation end to end → stuck users get fast help.

Pain point addressed

Stalled activation (BUG-2031) → first use isn't blocked.

2

Keep it, online

User action

Pays with card anywhere online → money stays in her account.

Internal state

Holds each purchase as pending → nothing debited until she decides.

Pain point addressed

Pay-now only (BUG-2060) → "decide first, pay later" restored.

3

Keep it, in store

User action

Taps phone at any till → same flexibility as online.

Internal state

Wallet and terminal acceptance run reliably → declines stop eroding trust.

Pain point addressed

Wallet and contactless declines (BUG-2049, BUG-2053) → debit fallback fades.

4

Decide and settle

User action

Taps keep, or returns item → pays only for what she keeps.

Internal state

Shows live spend, limit and due dates → no surprises or missed-payment fees.

Pain point addressed

Delayed transactions, silent repayment failures (BUG-2038, BUG-2044) → confidence replaces anxiety.

Why now: Klarna is already ahead in Germany and builds card habits every month. The Mastercard and Paymentology platform is already paid for. The Amazon Business pilot (October 2026) is absorbing capacity. CCD2 exemption sought.

Three advantages over the manual workaround

One place instead of many

Lena sees every pay-later purchase and due date in one app, instead of juggling Klarna, PayPal and a debit card.

Same behaviour everywhere

"Keep it first, pay later" works online and at the till, so she no longer falls back to pay-now in store.

Chosen, not defaulted

Lena picks Riverty deliberately instead of taking what the checkout offers, giving Riverty a direct relationship and interchange while merchants keep their checkout.

Two assumptions to confirm

  • Strategy wording: "Human-led curation" reads like it came from a streaming context. It was interpreted here as restoring brand authority through a dependable, transparent pay-later experience.
  • CCD2 exemption: Treated as something being sought, not yet granted, since the case brief lists CCD2 as a blocker for the Apple wallet slot. Stage 2's pay-later behaviour depends on how it is resolved.
