- **Write one feature that looks “correct” for the app but has zero strategic weight, you have no idea if it helps the company make money or whether a single user actually asked for it.:** Hydration score

A single 0-100 "hydration score" looks like a natural part of the Insights branch. It sounds smart, it makes the dashboard look polished, and any PM would nod at it in a feature review.

Here is why it has no strategic weight:

No evidence anyone asked for it. Users want to know whether they hit their goal, and the progress ring already answers that. Nothing suggests they were confused without a composite number.
No link to revenue. It doesn't drive subscriptions, retention, or conversion, and no one could say what a better score would earn us.
- **Compare V3 to your Step 1 baseline. What changed, and is the output objectively better, did the AI get smarter, or did your added PM knowledge (JTBD, effort/impact, retention) force it to be useful?:** What changed:

Structure. The baseline grouped features by type: logging, goals, reminders, insights, devices, engagement. V3 groups by decision: why users care, what's broken, what to build first, how to earn money, and what will make them leave.
Ranking. Baseline leaves were all equal. V3 puts one-tap logging and adaptive reminders as quick wins and calls the wearable auto-log a big bet that needs proof. The hydration score and smart bottles no longer get a slot by default.
Tensions. The baseline had none. V3 surfaces three: wearable bet vs. quick wins, paywall vs. habit growth, and nudging vs. annoying users.
User grounding. Every branch ties back to the target user (busy adults who forget), so features trace back to a problem.

The model organizes, sorts, and formats quickly, but it doesn't know which problem matters, which tradeoff your company should take, or whether a ranking is right.
